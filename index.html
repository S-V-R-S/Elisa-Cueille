Research Overview

2024 – 2027
Doctoral Thesis

Agent behavior generation under environmental constraints: application to prospective urban development scenarios

Metropolises today face rapid urban densification alongside pressing environmental and societal imperatives. The primary objective of my PhD research is to pioneer a novel simulation framework where individuals' activity chains are no longer merely calibrated or inferred from static historical survey data, but are dynamically generated based on both physical and social opportunities and constraints offered by an evolving environment.

Specifically, the research proposes a multimodal urban mobility model in which autonomous agents dynamically adapt their decisions, schedules, and transit paths to environmental feedback. This enables rigorous prospective assessment of future urban planning initiatives (such as Low Emission Zones (ZFE), new public transit lines, or infrastructure overhauls) on human behavior and city resilience.
